RAIL SCHEDULING AI — ENVIRONMENT ARCHITECTURE & JUSTIFICATION
=============================================================
File: environment/railway_env.py
Class: ModernizedLine104(gym.Env)
Last updated: Draft 2 (post-audit)

This document provides a comprehensive technical justification for every
major design decision in the Gymnasium environment.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CONCEPTUAL MODEL — THE CONVOY
------------------------------------------------------------------
The environment simulates two trains on a single-track line across the
76 km Polish Rail Line 104 (Chabówka → Nowy Sącz):

  Lead Train   — a deterministic "ghost" train that travels at a constant
                 configurable speed.  It is not controlled by the agent.
                 Its sole purpose is to create a moving safe-zone boundary
                 ahead of the AI train, forcing the agent to learn headway
                 management.  It is clamped to TRACK_END so it cannot
                 wander infinitely beyond the track and permanently lock
                 the signal to Green.

  AI Train     — the Gymnasium agent.  It receives a 7-dimensional
                 observation every second (DT=1.0 s) and outputs a
                 continuous acceleration command.  The Validation Layer
                 intercepts every command before physics are applied.

Justification for the convoy model:
  A single isolated train cannot learn headway or collision avoidance —
  there is nothing to follow.  Two trains on one track model the minimum
  plausible density for a safety-critical scheduling scenario and produce
  all the relevant signals (aspect, headway, DTZ) naturally from geometry.


------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TIMESTEP — DT = 1.0 SECOND
------------------------------------------------------------------
DT = 1.0 s is chosen to match ETCS Level 2 update semantics, where
movement authority is negotiated on a per-second basis (fact-check), and to keep the
Gymnasium step() call cheap.  At the maximum track speed (90 km/h =
25 m/s), a 1-second step advances the train at most 25 m — far smaller
than the minimum spatial headway of 34.7 m on S3–S5.  This guarantees
that no single step can silently skip an entire fixed block under normal
operating conditions.


------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ACTION SPACE — CONTINUOUS ACCELERATION  Box([-1.0], [0.5])
------------------------------------------------------------------
The agent outputs a single float representing the requested acceleration
(m/s²) for the coming second:

  -1.0  Emergency braking (maximum deceleration)
   0.0  Coasting (no traction, no braking)
  +0.5  Maximum traction

These bounds are dictated by physical limits of the rolling stock and
are identical to the constants used inside validate_layer/validator.py
(EMERGENCY_DECEL = -1.0, ACCEL = +0.5).  Keeping them in one place
(validator.py) and importing them avoids silent mismatches between the
safety layer's assumptions and what the environment actually allows.

A continuous action space is preferred over a discrete one because:
  a) It gives the PPO agent a gradient to follow rather than a lookup table.
  b) The Validation Layer's SUVAT-based override produces a continuously
     graduated correction, which is only meaningful if the original action
     is also continuous.
  c) It is physically realistic — a driver does not choose between four
     discrete brake levels; they apply a continuous pressure.


------------------------------------------------------------------
4. OBSERVATION SPACE — Box(7,)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Index  Feature                   Units    Range         Why included
-----  ------------------------  -------  ------------  ----------------------
  0    position                  m        [582, 76651]  Track co-ordinate frame
  1    speed                     m/s      [0, 30]       Core kinematic state
  2    dtz                       m        [0, 80000]    Distance to next block boundary; drives VL decisions
  3    signal_aspect             int      [0, 3]        ETCS 4-aspect signal; encodes lookahead clearance
  4    distance_to_next_station  m        [0, 80000]    Required for trapezoidal braking curve
  5    speed_limit               m/s      [0, 30]       Current segment limit from graph
  6    headway                   s        [0, 10000]    Temporal gap to lead train; core safety signal

Observation [6] (headway) is capped at 10 000 s, which matches the
declared space high of 10 000.0.  When the train is stationary (v < 0.01)
it is set to 10 000 rather than infinity to keep the observation finite
and within bounds.  The previous implementation used 9 999 in two
separate inline expressions that both disagreed with the declared space
high — this is now unified inside _compute_headway().


------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PHYSICS — SUVAT INTEGRATION
------------------------------------------------------------------
The environment uses SUVAT kinematics with DT = 1.0 s:

    Displacement:  Δx = u·t + ½·a·t²
    Velocity:      v  = u + a·t   (clamped to ≥ 0 and ≤ seg.limit_ms)

Where u = previous velocity, a = safe_a (the VL-approved acceleration).

Why use previous velocity (u) for Δx rather than post-step velocity?
  If you compute v_new first and then use v_new·t for displacement, you
  overestimate position on acceleration and underestimate on braking.
  This is the classic forward-Euler position error.  Using u·t + ½·a·t²
  (the exact SUVAT solution for constant acceleration over an interval)
  ensures the environment's physics are consistent with the Validation
  Layer's own two-phase SUVAT projections in _project().  If the two
  integration methods diverge, the VL will predict a different stopping
  point than the environment actually produces — causing spurious overrides
  on acceleration and missed overrides on braking.

Why is the segment looked up AFTER the position update?
  Each segment has its own speed limit.  If the segment is resolved before
  the position update, a train crossing a segment boundary in one step will
  have its speed clamped to the previous segment's limit, not the new one.
  This is particularly important at the S0→S2 transition (90 km/h → 60 km/h)
  where incorrect sequencing would allow the train to carry excess speed
  into the slower segment for one full second.

Physics constants are defined as class-level constants (ACCEL,
EMERGENCY_DECEL) and their values match validator.py.  The old name DECEL
has been replaced with EMERGENCY_DECEL to make this alignment explicit.


------------------------------------------------------------------
6. VALIDATION LAYER INTEGRATION
------------------------------------------------------------------
Every proposed action passes through the VL before physics run:

    safe_a, overridden = self.vl.get_safe_action(
        proposed_a, env_aspect, self.x, self.v, self.dtz
    )

The VL (validate_layer/validator.py) runs a 4-layer pipeline:

    Layer 1 — Ingestion & Dynamic Limits
              Maps the 4-aspect signal and DTZ to a maximum safe speed
              using v² = 2·a·s.

    Layer 2 — SUVAT Kinematic Simulation
              Projects the proposed trajectory at 33 %, 67 %, 100 % of
              t_stop = v_limit / |EMERGENCY_DECEL| and checks for spatial,
              kinematic, and segment-limit violations.  Lookahead is
              adaptive per segment, preventing false violations in short-
              block S3–S5 segments (SH = 34.7 m).

    Layer 3 — Graduated Override
              If unsafe: computes minimum required deceleration via
              a_needed = -u² / (2·s), clamped to [EMERGENCY_DECEL, 0].
              Only escalates to full emergency braking when s ≤ 0.1 m.

    Layer 4 — XAI Logging
              Every override is appended to override_log.csv with the
              original proposed action, corrected action, and constraint ID.

The environment treats the VL as a hard real-time interceptor — there is
no fallback if the VL raises an exception.  Any ValueError from
get_segment() means the train is off the track, which should terminate
the episode and is exposed via the collision / TRACK_END termination path.


------------------------------------------------------------------
7. LEAD TRAIN & SIGNAL ASPECT
------------------------------------------------------------------
The signal aspect is derived geometrically from the distance to the lead
train relative to the current segment's spatial headway (SH):

    dist > 3 × SH  →  Green        (3 blocks clear)
    dist > 2 × SH  →  Double-Yellow
    dist > 1 × SH  →  Yellow
    dist ≤ 1 × SH  →  Red          (next block occupied)

This mirrors ETCS Level 2 fixed-block semantics exactly.  SH is segment-
specific (312.5 m on S0/S1, 34.7 m on S3–S5), so the same physical gap
produces a different aspect depending on the line section — which is
correct: a 100 m gap is comfortable at 30 km/h but dangerous at 90 km/h.

The lead train is clamped to TRACK_END after each step:

    self.lead_x = min(lead_x + lead_speed * DT, TRACK_END)

Without this clamp, the lead train would travel hundreds of kilometres
beyond the track during a full 10 000-step episode at 20 m/s, making
dist_to_lead arbitrarily large and locking the signal permanently at
Green.  The agent would never encounter a non-Green aspect during training
and would fail to learn any braking or headway behaviour.


------------------------------------------------------------------
8. STATION DETECTION — WHILE LOOP
------------------------------------------------------------------
Station registration uses a while loop rather than a single if check:

    while self.last_station_idx + 1 < len(self.STATIONS):
        next_st_idx = self.last_station_idx + 1
        if self.x >= self.STATIONS[next_st_idx]:
            ...advance...
        else:
            break

With an if check, a train that skips two station thresholds in one step
(possible if DT is increased, or in unit tests that inject large
positions) would silently miss the intermediate station and incorrectly
leave it "unvisited".  The while loop is O(number of stations crossed
per step) — at most 6 per episode — so there is no performance cost.


------------------------------------------------------------------
9. HEADWAY COMPUTATION — _compute_headway()
------------------------------------------------------------------
Temporal headway (the time in seconds before the AI train reaches the
lead train's current position) is computed in a single place:

    if self.v > 0.01:
        return min((self.lead_x - self.x) / self.v, 10_000.0)
    return 10_000.0

The previous implementation duplicated this expression inline in both
step() and _get_obs() using a cap of 9 999.  This created two bugs:

  a) The observation-space declared high is 10 000.  Returning 9 999
     means a stationary train always produces an observation that is
     technically within bounds, but the declared maximum is never
     reachable by any real observation — misleading the normaliser.

  b) If the logic changed in one copy it would not update in the other.

The helper caps at 10 000 (matching the obs-space high), returns 10 000
when stationary (no divide-by-zero), and is called identically from both
step() and _get_obs().


------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TERMINATION & TRUNCATION
------------------------------------------------------------------
Terminated (natural end):
    a) self.x >= TRACK_END     — the AI train completes the route.
    b) reward_out.terminate    — the reward function raises a terminal
                                 condition (collision or critical headway
                                 violation).

Truncated (artificial cutoff):
    self.step_count >= MAX_STEPS (10 000 steps = ~2.8 hours simulated)

The distinction matters for PPO: terminated episodes carry a bootstrap
value of 0 (no future return beyond the terminal state); truncated
episodes carry a non-zero bootstrap because the agent could have continued
accruing reward.  Gymnasium's done = terminated | truncated convention is
respected.


------------------------------------------------------------------
11. INFO DICTIONARY
------------------------------------------------------------------
Every step returns a rich info dict:

    overridden       bool   Was the VL override triggered?
    safe_a           float  Physically executed acceleration
    proposed_a       float  Raw PPO output before VL
    aspect           int    Signal aspect at this step
    time             float  Simulated time (s)
    segment          str    Segment ID (S0–S5)
    reward_breakdown dict   Per-component reward values for diagnostics

This allows evaluate.py and any external analysis scripts to reconstruct
the full episode trajectory without re-running the simulation.
