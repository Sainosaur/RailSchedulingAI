STATIC vs DYNAMIC TRAPEZOID: REWARD PROFILE ANALYSIS
====================================================

You listed four primary objectives for your AI model:
1. Punctuality
2. Jerk Minimisation
3. Energy Efficiency
4. Maximise Speed

You are deciding between:
- STATIC: v_target = v_max * (d / D_b)   [Assuming D_b = 300m]
- DYNAMIC: v_target = sqrt(2 * a_comfort * d)  [Assuming a_comfort = 0.5 m/s²]

Below is the quantitative analysis demonstrating how each mathematical model handles these objectives depending on the track segment.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE 1: High Speeds (> 60 km/h) - E.g. Segments S0 & S1 (90 km/h / 25 m/s)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
In S0, the train must decelerate from 25 m/s to 0 m/s.

STATIC PROFILE
- Physics Check: To brake from 25 m/s to a full stop within exactly 300 meters, the train must decelerate at `1.04 m/s²` (derived from v² / 2s). 
- Result: 
   * Maximise Speed: ✅ Excellent. It forces the train to hold 90 km/h right up until the 300m mark.
   * Jerk & Energy: ❌ Fails miserably. A deceleration of `1.04 m/s²` is an emergency brake event. It ruins energy efficiency and creates massive jerk. The Validation Layer uses `1.0 m/s²` for emergency overrides, meaning the Static profile will cause the VL to override the RL agent constantly.

DYNAMIC PROFILE
- Physics Check: To brake at a comfortable `0.5 m/s²`, the dynamic formula tells the train to start braking exactly `625 meters` from the station boundary.
- Result:
   * Jerk & Energy: ✅ Excellent. The braking slope is completely smooth.
   * Maximise Speed: ⚠️ Sub-optimal. Because we prioritise comfort, the train begins losing speed 325 meters earlier than the static profile.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE 2: Medium Speeds (= 60 km/h) - E.g. Segment S2 (~17 m/s)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- At exactly 17 m/s, both equations calculate the exact same braking distance (300 meters). Both profiles perform identically on all 4 objectives in this segment.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
CASE 3: Low Speeds (< 60 km/h) - E.g. Segments S3, S4, S5 (30 km/h / 8.33 m/s)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
In S5, the train must decelerate from 8.33 m/s to 0.

STATIC PROFILE
- Physics Check: The static profile insists that the train begins braking 300 meters away. At 150 meters out, the static profile mathematically forces the train to go 4.1 m/s (14 km/h), crawling at a painfully slow speed.
- Result: 
   * Jerk & Energy: ❌ Poor. The train is forced to awkwardly throttle and drag itself over 300 meters at a crawl.
   * Maximise Speed & Punctuality: ❌ Fails miserably. The train is artificially delayed by hundreds of meters of crawling per station, destroying overall punctuality.

DYNAMIC PROFILE
- Physics Check: At `0.5 m/s²`, the dynamic mathematical curve calculates that only `69 meters` are needed to safely stop a train going 30 km/h.
- Result:
   * Jerk & Energy: ✅ Excellent. Smooth decel coast.
   * Maximise Speed & Punctuality: ✅ Excellent. The dynamic profile allows the train to cruise at the absolute top speed (30 km/h) right up until it is just 69 meters away. It entirely eliminates the static profile's 231 meters of wasted crawling.

====================================================
CONCLUSION: WHICH TO CHOOSE?
====================================================

If you try to "use both depending on the segment":
You could technically tell the AI to use Static for S0/S1 (to arrive faster) and Dynamic for S3-S5 (to prevent crawling). 
HOWEVER, using the Static equation on S0/S1 requires a deceleration of `1.04 m/s²`. Your Validation Layer considers `1.0 m/s²` an Emergency Stop. If the reward function attempts to use the static profile at 90km/h, the VL will intervene, trigger an emergency, and apply a massive penalty. 

VERDICT: CHOOSE ONE (DYNAMIC)
The Dynamic Equation (`sqrt(2*a*d)`) is universally superior because it is self-adjusting. 
You do not need to choose different equations for different segments. The dynamic equation mathematically tailors the exact perfect braking distance for *any* speed:
- If going 90 km/h, it automatically provides 625m of safe braking.
- If going 30 km/h, it automatically provides 69m of safe braking.

By making `a_comfort` a configurable variable (e.g., `0.5 m/s²` to `0.8 m/s²`), you give the RL agent the perfect mathematical envelope to satisfy all 4 objectives (Punctuality, Energy, Jerk, Max Speed) safely across the entire track without needing messy conditional logic per-segment.
