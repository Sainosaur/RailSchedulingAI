RAIL SCHEDULING AI: GYMNASIUM ENVIRONMENT DOCUMENTATION
=====================================================
File: environment/railway_env.py

This document explains the architecture, flow, and math behind the Reinforcement Learning (RL) custom environment. The `ModernizedLine104` class is built using the standard Gymnasium `gym.Env` framework, meaning it is perfectly compatible with RL libraries like Stable Baselines 3 (SB3) for PPO training.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CORE CONCEPT (THE CONVOY MODEL)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The environment simulates two trains running on a single track across the 76km Polish Rail Line 104 line. 
- The Lead Train: A "dummy" train that spawns ahead of our AI and cruises at a flat constant speed.
- The AI Train (The Agent): The train driven by the RL model trying to get from station to station quickly, smoothly, and safely without crashing into the lead train or violating ETCS speed bounds.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. AGENT COMMUNICATION (SPACES)
------------------------------------------------------------------

A. Observation Space (What the AI Sees)
A 7-dimensional Box matrix (array) of floats that feeds real-time telemetry to the AI every single second.
- [0] position: How many meters down the track the train is.
- [1] speed: Current velocity in m/s.
- [2] dtz (Distance-To-Zone): Distance to the next fixed-block ETCS boundary.
- [3] signal_aspect: The standard 4-aspect signal (0=Red, 1=Yellow, 2=Dbl-Yellow, 3=Green) showing how many blocks ahead are clear.
- [4] distance_to_next_station: Crucial for calculating the dynamic trapezoidal braking curve.
- [5] speed_limit: The maximum speed allowed in the current segment.
- [6] headway: The time gap separating the AI from the lead train in seconds.

B. Action Space (What the AI Does)
A continuous Box space with a single float in [-1.0, 0.5] representing the proposed acceleration (m/s²):
- -1.0: Emergency braking
-  0.0: Coast (no acceleration or braking)
- +0.5: Maximum traction acceleration

The agent outputs a raw acceleration request. The Validation Layer then checks
whether that acceleration is safe; if not, it overrides to emergency braking (-1.0).

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE SIMULATION LOOP (step() function)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The environment steps forward exactly 1 Second (DT = 1.0) at a time by executing the following 6 phases inside the `step()` function:

PHASE 1: The Validation Sieve
The environment hands the AI's requested continuous acceleration to the Validation Layer (`validator.py`). The VL runs a multi-step lookahead simulation (5, 10, 15 seconds) against the ETCS aspect boundaries. If the proposed acceleration would cause a spatial, temporal, kinematic, or segment-limit violation in the lookahead, the VL overrides the action to emergency braking (-1.0 m/s²). The absolute difference between the last executed acceleration and the new one is logged as `action_delta` to measure passenger jerk.

PHASE 2: Two-Phase SUVAT Physics
The environment applies the safe acceleration using a two-phase kinematic model that matches the Validation Layer's `_project()` function:
- If accelerating and the speed limit would be reached mid-step: accelerate to the limit, then coast for the remainder of the timestep.
- If braking and zero speed would be reached mid-step: brake to zero, then remain stationary for the remainder.
- Otherwise: standard SUVAT (s = ut + ½at²) for the full 1-second timestep.
This ensures the safety projection and actual physics are consistent — what the VL predicts is exactly what happens.

PHASE 3: Lead Train Advancement
The dummy lead train simply glides forward (`lead_speed * DT`). The environment then re-calculates the AI's `dtz` boundaries based on the new distance to the lead train.

PHASE 4: Station Checkmarks
If the AI Train's position crosses the coordinate boundary of the next target station, the station index shifts, unlocking the "Arrival Reward".

PHASE 5: Reward Calculation
The environment packages all the live data (speed, limits, headways, traction, override-flags) into the `TrainState` dataclass from `reward_function.py`. The Reward Function crunches the data and returns `total_reward`, composed of:
  - Continuous rewards (every step): progress, headway, speed profile, heartbeat
  - Event rewards (on occurrence): station arrival, punctuality, override penalty, jerk penalty, energy penalty
  - Terminal penalties (once, ends episode): headway violation, collision

PHASE 6: Termination & Truncation
- Termination occurs if the train finishes the 76km track, OR if it triggers a terminal penalty (collision or critical headway violation at 1x TH).
- Truncation (Max Steps) occurs if the simulated time hits 10,000 steps (~2.8 hours). This breaks the simulation artificially if an untrained AI decides to stall at 0 km/h forever, preventing the training software from hanging infinitely.
