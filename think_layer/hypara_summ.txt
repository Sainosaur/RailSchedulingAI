=== HYPERPARAMETER SUMMARY FOR RAIL SCHEDULING AI (PPO) ===

lr (Learning Rate)
What it is: How drastically the AI changes its belief system after making a mistake or earning a reward.
- High value (e.g., 0.001): Fast learning but highly unstable. May "forget" how to brake safely if it tries to avoid a penalty too aggressively.
- Low value (e.g., 0.00005): Slow, meticulous learning. Slower to train, but often creates a smoother driving curve.

batch_size
What it is: How many "memories" the AI studies at once before updating its brain.
- High value (e.g., 256): Very stable learning because it sees the big picture, but it takes more RAM.
- Low value (e.g., 64): High frequency of updates based on tiny data snippets. The learning curve will look erratic.

n_steps
What it is: Exactly how much simulated time/distance the AI plays out on the track before it tries to learn from the results.
- Value like 4096: The AI will drive a massive chunk of a journey (e.g., leaving a station, navigating a hazard, and arriving at the next station) *before* stopping to think about if what it did was good. Highly recommended for train networks because braking consequences take thousands of meters to play out.

gamma (Discount Factor)
What it is: The AI's "foresight" or care about the future.
- 0.99: The AI only cares about consequences roughly 100 steps into the future. 
- 0.999 or 0.9999: The AI cares about consequences 1000 to 10000 steps into the future. Because commercial trains take kilometers to stop, higher gammas natively force the AI to respect physical consequences minutes in advance.

ent_coef (Entropy Coefficient)
What it is: The algorithm's built-in "curiosity" module.
- High value (e.g., 0.1): The AI will frequently ignore what it thinks is the safest move and instead try something wild (like speeding) just to test boundaries. 
- Low value (e.g., 0.001): Highly risk-averse. Once it discovers driving slowly works, it may never explore faster speeds to earn progression rewards.

net_arch (Network Architecture)
What it is: The physical size of the mathematical hidden layers.
- small ([64, 64]): Forces the AI to generalize and learn the physics concepts of momentum and stopping distance rather than overfitting.
- medium ([128, 128]): Capable of learning more complex multi-variable actions, but runs a high risk of memorizing the exact locations of the stations rather than understanding *why* it should stop.

=== OPTUNA EVALUATION METRICS ===

1. Why are early Mean Rewards negative (-500)?
In the line 104 simulation, an AI loses -20 points every time the Validation Layer emergency-overrides a dangerous move. A completely untrained AI will get thousands of penalties, scoring -20,000. 
If an Optuna trial hits -500, that is actually a massive improvement—it means the AI is successfully making the 76km journey and only making a handful of mistakes.
To get a fully positive Mean Reward (e.g. +500 for a flawless run with 0 infractions), you must train the winning configuration on a massive 3,000,000+ step run with train.py. Optuna's goal is merely figuring out which config learns fastest in 100k test steps.

2. Why are the hyperparameters so absurdly precise? (e.g. 1.03003399...e-05)
Optuna does not think in human-readable constants. It models complex, multi-dimensional probability curves. When picking a variable to test, it extracts the absolute exact floating-point coordinate on the topological curve that possesses the highest mathematical certainty of yielding an improvement.

3. What is a "Step"?
A mathematical "Step" does not mean a chunk of track length. In our environment, 1 Step = 1 Simulated Second of physical time. Because it takes roughly 4,000 real-world simulated seconds for a train to drive 76 kilometers, 3,000,000 steps simply means the environment forces the AI to drive the entire route over 750 times on hyper-speed until it masters the physics.

4. Is the AI a baby at the start of the live Simulation?
No. During the `train.py` run, the AI acts like a baby exploring for the first time. But once it finishes those 3,000,000 steps, its mature, expert brain is frozen and saved into `best_model.zip`. When you launch the web dashboard, it loads this frozen adult brain. It does absolutely zero learning or exploring on the live dashboard tracks—it is deployed as a master from Segment 0.

=== STABLE BASELINES 3 TRAINING LOGS (TERMINAL) ===

TIME /
- fps: Frames Per Second. How many simulation steps the AI is calculating every wall-clock second. E.g., ~1100 fps means your system is processing 1,100 simulated seconds (steps) of train movement every real-world second.
- iterations: The number of times the AI has collected `n_steps` amount of data and run an update on its underlying neural network.
- time_elapsed: Total real-world wall-clock time spent training so far.
- total_timesteps: Total simulation steps the AI has experienced overall across the entire training run.

ROLLOUT /
- ep_len_mean: The average length of the environment episodes. In our case, a longer episode means it either reached the end of the line or drove slower.
- ep_rew_mean: Mean Episode Reward. This is the single most important metric. You want to see this start very negative and climb up closer to 0 or positive as the AI learns to avoid penalties and earn progression rewards.

TRAIN /
- approx_kl: Measures how drastically the AI changed its own "brain" during this update. If this spikes extremely high, the AI took too big of a leap and might destabilize.
- clip_fraction: How frequently the PPO algorithm had to mathematically block the AI from making updates that were too suspiciously massive.
- clip_range: The limit set for how much the AI's internal policy probabilities can change per update (often 0.2).
- entropy_loss: How much random chaotic "exploration / curiosity" the AI is doing. This naturally drops over time as the AI gets confident in its optimal strategy.
- explained_variance: How well the AI's "Critic" module is predicting the future value of the AI's actions. Closer to 1.0 means the AI is perfectly predicting the future consequences; 0 or negative means the AI is heavily confused.
- learning_rate: Current learning multiplier.
- loss: Overall mathematical measure of the AI's prediction errors (you generally want to see this decrease over time).
- policy_gradient_loss: How efficiently the AI is learning to push actions that yield higher rewards and abandon actions that cause penalties.
- value_loss: Error rate of the AI's "Critic" network in guessing how good a certain track state was.
